# Creative direction — Why Airlines Sell More Tickets Than Seats

Create a fast, witty, English-language visual explainer for YouTube about airline overbooking.

## Core promise

The viewer should feel like they discovered a hidden system, not attended a lesson. Never explain a concept before creating curiosity for it.

## Tone

Smart, fast, playful, adult, slightly dry humor. No meme spam, no childish cartoon energy, no classroom framing. The narrator should sound amused by the system, not angry at airlines.

Humor should come from phrasing and visual contrast: calm corporate math colliding with very human airport chaos.

## Visual system

Faceless and fully graphic-driven. Use clean vector diagrams, seat grids, tickets, boarding passes, simple human silhouettes, airport signage, probability counters, route cards, and kinetic typography.

Preferred look: modern editorial explainer, dark navy / warm off-white base, restrained bright accents for tickets, warnings, and probability changes. High contrast, large typography, generous negative space.

No airline logos. No stock footage is required. Avoid dense dashboards and tiny text.

## Story structure

1. Hook: 180 seats, 185 tickets. "No, nobody failed kindergarten."
2. Reveal the missing variable: predictable no-shows and expiring seat inventory.
3. Run a simple visual simulation: 185 tickets, different numbers of no-shows.
4. Escalate to the failure case: everyone shows up.
5. Show volunteers / compensation as the system's pressure-release valve.
6. Reveal the real optimization problem: empty-seat cost vs overbooking cost.
7. Close on the deeper insight: airlines are managing probabilities around seats.

## Pacing rules

Target roughly 2:30–3:00.

Create a meaningful visual change every 3–7 seconds and a new story beat roughly every 10–20 seconds. Use motion to reveal information progressively instead of placing full diagrams on screen at once.

The first 15 seconds must work with zero context. Do not open with a logo, channel intro, definition, or history.

## Signature visual beats

- Seat grid counts rapidly to 180, then five extra tickets slide in and physically have nowhere to sit.
- Empty seat turns into a timer and then a giant $0 at departure.
- Three quick simulations: seven no-shows / five no-shows / zero no-shows.
- On zero no-shows, five extra passenger icons remain standing while every seat is filled. On-screen label: "statistical error has entered the chat."
- Volunteer compensation appears like an auction counter rising while one passenger slowly raises a hand.
- Final shot zooms out from one seat into a probability model, then collapses back to a simple plane icon.

## Accuracy guardrails

- Overbooking is not universal; some airlines do not oversell.
- Describe no-show numbers in the script as hypothetical examples, not industry averages.
- Airlines use revenue-management / forecasting systems and historical behavior; do not claim a universal proprietary formula.
- Passenger rights vary by jurisdiction. The U.S. DOT requires airlines to seek volunteers before involuntary denied boarding due to oversales; do not imply that exact rule is global.

## Packaging

Primary title: **Why Airlines Sell More Tickets Than Seats**

Alternate title: **Your Flight Has 180 Seats. So Why Did They Sell 185 Tickets?**

Thumbnail concept: top-down 180-seat grid, five red ticket icons floating outside it, large minimal text: **180 SEATS / 185 TICKETS?!**

## Audio

One English narrator. Conversational, confident, quick but never rushed. Dry comedic timing. Tiny pauses before punchlines and reveals. Soundtrack remains disabled for this first test so narration and visual rhythm carry the piece.

## QA priorities

The video must never look like a slideshow or lecture deck. Every diagram should tell a story through motion. Keep numbers readable, avoid overlapping seat/ticket icons, keep all jokes visually legible, and ensure every final-hold frame looks intentional.
